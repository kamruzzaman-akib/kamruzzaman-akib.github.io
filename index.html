About me

Hands-on, documentation-driven, and always asking “why did this happen?”

I’m a Systems Administrator at Syndigo in Lisbon. I look after the full user lifecycle across Active Directory, Entra ID, Google Workspace and Atlassian Cloud, manage Windows and macOS devices with Microsoft Intune, and support platform and tenant migrations. I joined 1WorldSync as a Desktop Support Engineer in December 2024 and moved into systems administration when Syndigo acquired the company in September 2025.

My route into IT administration ran through field testing and technical support in Bangladesh, the Netherlands and Portugal. That testing background still shapes how I work: before a change reaches users, I build realistic scenarios in virtual machines, try to break things, and share what I find with the team.

Helpdesk work taught me that closing a ticket isn’t enough. I look for the root cause and ask how to stop it coming back, a habit I call “automating a task without using automation”. And when I solve something new, I write it up straight away so nobody has to reinvent the wheel.

How I work

Four habits behind everything I do

01

A better experience for everyone

When I pick up a ticket, the person knows what I’m doing, what comes next and how long it will take. Clear expectations build trust, and over time colleagues started coming to me directly.

02

Fix the cause, not just the ticket

Every recurring issue is a prompt to improve a process, a configuration or a piece of documentation so it doesn’t happen again.

03

Test before production

Coming from field testing, I validate changes in virtual machines against real-life scenarios before they reach users, then share the results with the team.

04

Write it down

Context, steps and screenshots go into the knowledge base the moment a solution works, so the whole team can reuse it.

The folder nobody could delete

A colleague’s laptop had a large C:\wim folder eating his disk space, and nobody knew how to remove it. Digging through our documentation, I worked out it was left over from mounting a Windows image to inject drivers. I built a bootable USB, used it to unmount the image, and the folder could finally be deleted. Then I wrote the fix up for the team.

A same-day hardware swap

A colleague had put up with a slow, noisy laptop for weeks, assuming a fix would take ages. On a quick call I traced the symptoms and the whining fan coil to a failing motherboard, went into the office that day and shipped him a replacement. He was back to full speed almost immediately.
